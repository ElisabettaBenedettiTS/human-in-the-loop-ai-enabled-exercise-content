# Synthetic Case Study 03 — Post-Exercise Review and Lessons Capture

## Purpose
Demonstrate how AI-enabled tools can support post-exercise analysis without replacing human interpretation, assessment, or lessons ownership.

All details are fictional and unclassified.

## Exercise Context
A fictional multinational logistics headquarters completed a training event involving disruption to a cross-border sustainment corridor.

During execution, the Training Audience received:
- a host-nation restriction on heavy military movement;
- competing fuel and medical resupply requirements;
- an alternate route with increased transit time.

The Training Audience eventually selected a prioritisation option and coordinated with host-nation stakeholders.

## Post-Exercise Objective
Assess whether the content:
- generated the intended decision activity;
- created appropriate operational pressure;
- preserved decision space;
- supported Training Objective achievement;
- contained avoidable ambiguity;
- should be retained, revised, or replaced.

## Synthetic Observation Notes
- Training Audience identified competing priorities quickly.
- Initial host-nation coordination was delayed.
- Medical requirement dominated discussion early.
- Fuel impact was not quantified until later.
- Alternate route was considered only after facilitator prompting.
- Training Audience requested clarification on whether the restriction applied to all vehicles or heavy vehicles only.
- EXCON assessed that the trigger created useful friction.
- One observer believed the host-nation notification was too ambiguous.
- No significant doctrinal issue was identified.
- Training Objective judged partially achieved during the first decision cycle and fully achieved after refinement.

## AI-Assisted Post-Exercise Analysis
### What Worked
- Competing sustainment demands generated a credible prioritisation problem.
- The scenario created enough pressure to stimulate coordination.
- The alternate route provided a realistic second-order option.

### What Did Not Work
- Restriction wording created avoidable ambiguity.
- The alternate route was not sufficiently visible initially.
- Fuel-impact information may have been introduced too late.

### Potential Lesson
Distinguish between:
- intentional ambiguity designed to create training friction;
- accidental ambiguity that distracts from the Training Objective.

### Recommended Changes
- Clarify that the restriction applies to heavy military vehicles.
- Preserve uncertainty about prioritisation.
- Ensure route-comparison data can be accessed when appropriately requested.

## Human Review
The reviewer accepted the ambiguity distinction but rejected an AI suggestion to provide fuel-impact data automatically.

Reason: giving the data too early could reduce the requirement for the Training Audience to identify its own information needs.

Final human recommendation:
> Ensure fuel-impact information is available through an appropriate information request or supporting product, rather than automatically presenting it at the start.

## Final Human-Reviewed Lessons
### Retain
- Competing sustainment priorities
- Host-nation coordination requirement
- Alternate-route option
- Decision pressure

### Revise
- Clarify scope of the host-nation restriction
- Keep supporting information discoverable rather than automatically exposed
- Make known facts vs unknowns more explicit

### Avoid
- Providing all quantitative data automatically
- Removing productive ambiguity
- Turning the content into a scripted solution path

## Key Takeaway
AI can support post-exercise synthesis and lessons capture, but human reviewers remain responsible for interpreting causality, training effect, and design intent.
