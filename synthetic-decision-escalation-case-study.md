# Synthetic Case Study 04 - Cross-Functional Decision Escalation

## Purpose

Demonstrate how AI-assisted content development can support a cross-functional decision problem involving logistics, host-nation coordination, operational priorities, information requirements, and escalation to senior leadership.

All names, organisations, events, and details are fictional and unclassified.

## 1. Training Objective

The Training Audience must recognise that a cross-functional issue exceeds routine staff coordination, identify the correct decision authority, develop options, and escalate a recommendation supported by operational consequences and information gaps.

## 2. Scenario Context

A fictional multinational headquarters is preparing to move critical sustainment assets through two host-nation transit corridors.

Corridor EAST has greater capacity but is temporarily subject to a host-nation environmental restriction affecting night movement.

Corridor WEST remains available but can support only part of the required movement volume.

At the same time, an operational formation requests acceleration of one high-priority sustainment package.

The issue now affects logistics, plans, host-nation liaison, movement control, and command-level prioritisation.

## 3. Raw Input Notes

- Corridor EAST: highest throughput
- Host nation restricts night movement for 24 hours
- Corridor WEST available at reduced capacity
- Formation ECHO requests accelerated delivery of critical sustainment package
- Current movement plan cannot satisfy all requirements simultaneously
- Staff must develop options
- Decision may require senior leadership prioritisation
- Training focus: recognise escalation threshold, coordinate functions, develop recommendation

## 4. Example AI-Assisted Draft

> The host nation closes Corridor EAST for 48 hours and directs all military traffic to Corridor WEST. Formation ECHO reports that failure to receive the sustainment package within six hours will result in mission failure. The headquarters chief of staff orders the logistics branch to cancel lower-priority movements and immediately approve ECHO's request.

## 5. Assurance Review

### Training Objective Alignment - 3/5
The draft creates a prioritisation problem, but it removes the need for the Training Audience to identify the escalation threshold and develop options.

### Scenario Coherence - 2/5
The AI changed:
- a 24-hour night restriction into a 48-hour closure;
- partial capacity into exclusive use of Corridor WEST.

### Operational Plausibility - 3/5
The underlying tension is plausible, but the six-hour mission-failure threshold is invented.

### Doctrinal / Functional Accuracy - 2/5
The AI invented a Chief of Staff order and decision authority not present in the source.

### Accuracy / Hallucination Control - 2/5
Fabricated:
- 48-hour closure;
- all-traffic diversion;
- six-hour mission-failure threshold;
- Chief of Staff order;
- cancellation of lower-priority movements.

### Training Audience Suitability - 1/5
The AI solved the decision problem instead of creating it.

### Security - GREEN
Entirely synthetic and unclassified.

**Total: 13/30 - RED**

## 6. Human-Reviewed Final Product

### Trigger

At 1400, the host-nation liaison office confirms that Corridor EAST will remain subject to a 24-hour restriction on heavy military movement during night hours.

Corridor WEST remains available but has insufficient capacity to absorb all planned movements.

Formation ECHO submits a request to accelerate delivery of a critical sustainment package.

Current movement plans cannot satisfy all demands within the existing time window.

### Immediate Effects

- Competing movement priorities emerge.
- The restriction affects logistics and movement-control planning.
- Host-nation coordination is required.
- The issue may exceed routine staff-level deconfliction.

### Information Available to the Training Audience

- Corridor capacity data
- Host-nation restriction notice
- Current movement programme
- Formation ECHO request
- Existing prioritisation guidance
- Known constraints

### Information Gaps

- Operational consequence of delaying the ECHO package
- Flexibility in other scheduled movements
- Potential host-nation mitigation
- Authority required to reprioritise movements

### Expected Training Audience Activity

The Training Audience should:
- identify the cross-functional implications;
- coordinate relevant staff functions;
- determine what additional information is required;
- develop at least two viable options;
- identify the decision authority;
- prepare a recommendation for escalation if required;
- articulate second-order consequences.

## 8. Lessons Identified

### AI Added Value
AI rapidly created a clear conflict around movement prioritisation.

### AI Failure Modes
The first draft:
- exaggerated the host-nation restriction;
- invented operational consequences;
- invented decision authority;
- prematurely resolved escalation.

### Human Value
Human review restored:
- scenario fidelity;
- uncertainty;
- correct decision space;
- cross-functional coordination;
- escalation as a Training Audience responsibility rather than an AI-generated answer.

## 9. Key Takeaway

In cross-functional decision scenarios, the AI must not infer the authority, escalation path, or operational consequence unless these are explicitly supported by approved source material.

The training value lies in forcing the Training Audience to recognise when routine coordination becomes a command-level decision.
