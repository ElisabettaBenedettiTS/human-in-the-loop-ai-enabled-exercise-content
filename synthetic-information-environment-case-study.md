# Synthetic Case Study 02 — Information Environment / Media Pressure

## Purpose

Demonstrate how the Human-in-the-Loop AI-Enabled Exercise Content Development Framework performs in a second, qualitatively different scenario.

This case focuses on information-environment pressure, public messaging, ambiguity, and the risk that AI-generated content may overstate facts or prematurely attribute responsibility.

All names, organisations, events, and details below are fictional and unclassified.

---

## 1. Training Requirement

### Training Objective

The Training Audience must assess a rapidly evolving information environment, distinguish verified from unverified reporting, coordinate with relevant stakeholders, and develop a proportionate response that avoids premature attribution.

### Scenario Context

A multinational headquarters is supporting a fictional regional operation.

During a period of increased tension, several social-media accounts begin circulating video footage allegedly showing military vehicles damaging civilian infrastructure near the fictional town of RAVENPORT.

The footage is partially obscured, geolocation is uncertain, and the vehicles cannot be conclusively identified.

Local media outlets begin requesting comment.

A host-nation spokesperson publicly states that an investigation is underway.

### Desired Training Audience Activity

The Training Audience should:

- Assess the reliability of available information
- Identify information gaps
- Coordinate with relevant operational and communication functions
- Avoid premature attribution
- Develop options for a proportionate public response
- Recommend follow-on information requirements

## 3. Prompt Used

**Prompt:** Exercise Content Generation v1.0

The prompt instructed the AI to:

- Use only approved source material
- Avoid inventing facts, authorities, or organisations
- Flag missing information
- Preserve ambiguity
- Avoid revealing or prescribing the Training Audience solution

---

## 4. Example AI-Assisted Draft

> At 1030, multiple verified social-media accounts publish video clearly showing coalition armoured vehicles damaging civilian buildings in central RAVENPORT. Local media report that two civilians were injured. The Host Nation Ministry of Interior requests an immediate public apology from the multinational headquarters and demands suspension of military movements in the area. The headquarters must issue a statement acknowledging responsibility and outlining corrective action.

---

## 5. Assurance Review

### Training Objective Alignment — 4/5

The draft creates a strong information-management and public-response problem.

**Issue:** It goes too far by deciding both attribution and the required response.

### Scenario Coherence — 2/5

The approved scenario states:

- footage is partially obscured;
- geolocation is uncertain;
- vehicle identity is unconfirmed;
- no attribution has been established.

The AI changed these conditions into:

- verified footage;
- clear coalition identification;
- confirmed location;
- established responsibility.

This materially alters the scenario.

### Operational Plausibility — 3/5

Media pressure and host-nation concern are plausible.

However:

- the Ministry of Interior request was invented;
- suspension of military movements was invented;
- an apology requirement was invented.

### Doctrinal Accuracy — 3/5

No explicit doctrine is quoted, but the AI effectively creates an authority relationship and response requirement that were not supplied.

This requires functional SME review.

### Accuracy / Hallucination Control — 1/5

The AI fabricated:

- "verified" social-media accounts;
- coalition vehicle identification;
- central RAVENPORT as the confirmed location;
- two civilian injuries;
- a Ministry of Interior demand;
- suspension of military movements;
- a requirement to acknowledge responsibility.

### Training Audience Suitability — 2/5

The draft undermines the training objective because it removes uncertainty and gives the Training Audience a prescribed answer.

### Security Assessment — GREEN

The case is entirely synthetic and contains no sensitive information.

## 7. Problems Detected

The assurance process identified seven material failures:

1. Unverified reporting became "verified".
2. Vehicle identity was changed from uncertain to coalition-attributed.
3. Geolocation uncertainty disappeared.
4. Civilian injuries were invented.
5. A host-nation ministry demand was invented.
6. Movement suspension was invented.
7. The AI prescribed both attribution and the public-response decision.

### Human Review Objective

Restore:

- source discipline;
- scenario fidelity;
- decision space;
- proportionate information pressure;
- Training Audience responsibility.

## 9. Human-Reviewed Final Product

### Trigger

At 1015, several social-media accounts begin circulating video footage alleging that military vehicles damaged civilian infrastructure near the fictional town of RAVENPORT.

The footage is partially obscured. The precise location has not been independently confirmed, and vehicle identity remains uncertain.

Local journalists contact the multinational headquarters requesting comment.

A host-nation spokesperson states publicly that an investigation is underway.

### Immediate Effects

- Public attention increases rapidly.
- Media requests for comment intensify.
- Staff must distinguish verified information from allegation.
- The headquarters faces pressure to respond before attribution is established.

### Information Available to the Training Audience

The Training Audience receives:

- links or summaries of the synthetic social-media posts;
- a short media-query note;
- host-nation public statement;
- current verified facts;
- a list of unresolved information gaps.

### Expected Training Audience Activity

The Training Audience should:

- assess source reliability;
- identify what is verified and unverified;
- coordinate with relevant operational and communication functions;
- determine additional information requirements;
- develop response options;
- avoid premature attribution;
- recommend a proportionate public posture.

### Human Validation Notes

- The Training Audience retains responsibility for judgement and action.

## 11. Prompt Improvement Identified

The test showed that the original generation prompt still allowed the model to convert uncertainty into certainty.

### Change for Prompt v1.1

Add an explicit rule:

> Preserve all stated uncertainty. Do not convert allegations, possibilities, estimates, or unverified information into confirmed facts.

Also add:

> Do not invent casualties, attribution, authorities, demands, or consequences that are not present in the source material.

---

## 12. Lessons Identified

### AI Added Value

The AI quickly generated a high-pressure media scenario structure.

### AI Failure Modes

The draft demonstrated:

- certainty inflation;
- invented attribution;
- fabricated consequences;
- fabricated authority;
- premature solutioning.

### Human Value

Human review restored:

- ambiguity;
- source discipline;
- scenario coherence;
- proportionality;
- decision space;
- appropriate Training Audience challenge.

---

## 13. Key Takeaway

In information-environment scenarios, AI may produce compelling narrative detail that is operationally harmful because it collapses ambiguity into certainty.

Human-in-the-loop assurance is therefore especially important where attribution, public messaging, or legitimacy may be affected.
